# Photography sources, licences and production blockers

**Status: NOT production-cleared.** Read the blocker section before shipping these images.

The 2026-11 metallic redesign replaced the generated isometric illustrations on the public
marketing surfaces with photographs of real hosting infrastructure. This document records where
those photographs came from, what was done to them, and — importantly — what has **not** yet been
verified.

Related files:

- `shared/site/photography.json` — the machine-readable registry (11 photos, family → photo map,
  alt text, credits). This is the source of truth for `node scripts/site/generate.mjs`, which emits
  `templates/cloudhost247/includes/photo-map.tpl` and
  `cloudhost247-node/frontend/src/components/marketing/photo-map.generated.ts`.
- `assets/images/cloudhost247/photography/sources.json` — per-photo source preview filename and
  the source page / photo identifier recorded by the image search.
- `assets/images/cloudhost247/photography/*.jpg` — 1280 px-wide masters. Each has a 640 / 960 /
  1280 AVIF + WebP ladder generated by `scripts/generate-raster-formats.py` and mirrored to
  `cloudhost247-node/frontend/public/media/cloudhost247/photography/`.
- `image-search/` — local working copies of the downloaded search previews. **Gitignored**; these
  are acquisition intermediates, not release assets.

## What these images are

Real photographs of enterprise data-centre infrastructure: server aisles, rack-mounted hardware,
fibre patch panels, structured cabling, network switches, storage arrays, engineers at work and a
network operations centre. They are **not** illustrations, renders, AI-generated art or glowing
abstract technology imagery. Every one is mapped to a hosting product family in
`photography.json` (for example `servers` → rack hardware, `security` → operations centre,
`domains` → fibre patch panel), so the picture on a page relates to the service on that page.

The SVG isometric artwork was deliberately kept as the final `<source>`/`<img>` fallback in
`visual.tpl` and `Illustration.tsx`. If a photo fails, the page still renders a coherent
infrastructure scene; it never renders a broken image.

## Processing applied

The acquisition step produced **500 px-class search previews**. Those were resized to 1280 px width
with ImageMagick (Lanczos filter, mild unsharp, light sigmoidal contrast grade, JPEG quality 84),
then encoded into the 640 / 960 / 1280 AVIF + WebP ladder. No content was generated, composited or
painted over. Metadata-only crops/grades do not change the subject.

## Blocker: replace before production

1. **Resolution.** The 1280 px masters are upscaled previews, not full-resolution originals. On a
   2× display at the 664 px rendered width the 1280 rung is adequate, but the underlying detail is
   not. Download the original file for each of the 11 photos from its verified source page and
   regenerate the ladder with `python3 scripts/generate-raster-formats.py`.
2. **Licence verification.** Open each source page, confirm the photographer and the exact licence
   terms, and correct any mismatched identifier (especially `server-racks-blue`). Record the
   verified URL and photographer in `photography.json` and in the table above.
3. **Model / property releases.** Photographs showing identifiable engineers or branded equipment
   may need releases for commercial use. Confirm this on a per-photo basis.
4. **No watermark.** The local previews were inspected and none showed a visible watermark, but
   this must be re-checked on the full-resolution originals. Watermarked Vecteezy / Getty /
   illustration candidates were rejected during acquisition and are not in the shipped set.

Until all four items are closed, these photographs are **staging/demo assets**. The design, the
resolver, the responsive ladder and the fallback chain are production-quality; the bitmaps are not.
No photo is used inside the customer account, cart, checkout, dashboard or provisioning surfaces —
those remain unchanged and do not depend on this library.

## How to swap a photo

1. Replace `assets/images/cloudhost247/photography/<key>.jpg` with the verified ≥1280 px master.
2. Update the `credit` / `alt` in `shared/site/photography.json` if the source changed.
3. `python3 scripts/generate-raster-formats.py` (regenerates the ladder), then
   `python3 scripts/generate-raster-formats.py --emit-served` (mirrors to the SPA), then
   `node scripts/site/generate.mjs` (re-emits both photo maps).
4. `python3 scripts/generate-raster-formats.py --check` and `python3 -m unittest discover -s
   tests/website -p 'test_*.py' -q`.
